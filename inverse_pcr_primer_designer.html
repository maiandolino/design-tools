Inverse-PCR Primer Design Tool

Design divergent primers for site-directed mutagenesis by inverse PCR, with three re-circularization strategies: Golden Gate (Type IIS site + 4-nt overhang), Gibson (overlapping 20–40 bp 5′ ends) or Simple (flanking primers, no added DNA). Nearest-neighbor Tm (SantaLucia 1998 + Owczarzy 2008 salt correction); forward and reverse primers are matched to within your ΔTm limit.

Gibson overlap design goals — these govern the assembly only; the PCR itself is governed by the whole-primer annealing Tm above. Defaults: 20–40 bp, Tm 60–70 °C optimal, 40–60 % GC, G/C cap at 3′ end. Hard limits: below 15 bp assembly is prone to failure, and the overlap Tm must clear the 50 °C reaction temperature (48 °C floor); past 40 bp costs more without assembling better.
How the primers are built & how Tm is computed

Golden Gate. Each primer is built 5'→3' as: [filler][recognition site][spacer (1 nt)][overhang (4 nt)][3′ arm]. BsaI-HFv2 cuts GGTCTC(1/5), so after the recognition site and 1 spacer nt the next 4 nt become the 5' overhang. The forward and reverse overhangs are exact reverse-complements of the same clean 4-bp fusion site, so the linear inverse-PCR product re-circularizes seamlessly. The mutation is placed in the 3′ arm of one primer (never in the overhang), with at least "Min matched flank" bases of perfectly-matched template on both sides of the mutation so the mismatched primer still anneals well. The fusion site is searched from that minimum out to +"Fusion-site search range".

Gibson. Both primers are pure template sequence whose 5′ ends overlap by 20–40 bp: Fwd = [overlap][3′ extension →] and Rev = [rev-comp of overlap][← 3′ extension]. The shared overlap is duplicated at both ends of the linear inverse-PCR product, so Gibson (5′ exonuclease / polymerase / ligase) joins the ends into the circular plasmid with no scar. Candidate overlaps are scanned (every length and position that satisfies your mutation-placement rule) and scored against your goals (length, Tm, GC %, G/C cap — all editable). Because every base of a Gibson primer is template, the whole primer anneals: the annealing Tm reported for the PCR is computed over the overlap and the 3′ extension together, as one oligo. The overlap's own Tm is a separate, assembly-stage number. The 3′ extension then only has to carry the primer into your annealing-Tm window, so it is often just a few nucleotides. The mutation can sit inside the overlap (both primers carry it, QuikChange-like) or in the 3′ extension of one primer (the overlap stays wild-type, with ≥ "Min matched flank" matched bases between the overlap and the mutation). A G/C cap means the overlap starts and ends with G/C, so the 3′ ends that anneal after exonuclease chew-back are GC-clamped.

Simple inverse PCR. The two primers are back-to-back (divergent) at a junction in the template and carry no additional DNA. The mutation sits inside one primer with ≥ "Min matched flank" matched bases on both sides. The product is a blunt linear amplicon: order the primers 5′-phosphorylated (or phosphorylate with PNK) and re-circularize with blunt ligation / KLD after DpnI digestion.

Annealing Tm is computed over every base of the primer that matches the template — that includes the 4-nt Golden Gate overhang and the Gibson overlap, because both are slices of the template and therefore anneal; only the Golden Gate filler + recognition site + spacer are true non-templated 5′ tails and are excluded. Tm uses SantaLucia 1998 nearest-neighbor ΔH/ΔS with Owczarzy 2008 monovalent+divalent salt correction; free Mg²⁺ = [Mg²⁺] − [dNTP]. It is evaluated on the primer's own bases (the perfect-match Tm against the product made in cycle 2+). The annealing Tm window is a hard constraint: only lengths that put the whole primer inside it are considered, and of those the shortest pair that also respects your ΔTm limit is chosen, so the 3′ length minimum is only a floor — set the Tm you want and let the extension be as short as it needs to be. The reported 5′/3′ matched flanks are the runs of perfectly-matched bases on each side of the mutation that pair with the original template in cycle 1.
