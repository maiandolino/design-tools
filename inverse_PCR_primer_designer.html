Inverse-PCR Primer Design Tool

Design divergent primers for site-directed mutagenesis via inverse PCR with three re-circularization strategies: Golden Gate (Type IIS site + 4-nt overhang), Gibson (overlapping 15–25 bp 5′ ends) or Simple (flanking primers, no added DNA). Nearest-neighbor Tm (SantaLucia 1998 + Owczarzy 2008 salt correction) is computed for annealing regions, overhangs and Gibson overlaps.

How the primers are built & how Tm is computed

Golden Gate. Each primer is built 5'→3' as: [filler][recognition site][spacer (1 nt)][overhang (4 nt)][annealing]. For example, BsaI-HFv2 cuts GGTCTC(1/5), so after the recognition site and 1 spacer nt the next 4 nt become the 5' overhang. The forward and reverse overhangs are exact reverse-complements of the same clean 4-bp fusion site, so the linear inverse-PCR product re-circularizes seamlessly. The mutation is placed in the annealing region of one primer (never in the overhang), with at least "Min matched flank" bases of perfectly-matched template on both sides of the mutation so the mismatched primer still anneals well. The fusion site is searched from that minimum out to +"Fusion-site search range" to find a suitable overhang.

Gibson. Both primers are pure template sequence whose 5′ ends overlap by 15–25 bp: Fwd = [overlap][3′ extension →] and Rev = [rev-comp of overlap][← 3′ extension]. The shared overlap is duplicated at both ends of the linear inverse-PCR product, so Gibson (5′ exonuclease / polymerase / ligase) joins the ends into the circular plasmid with no scar. Candidate overlaps are scanned (every length and position that satisfies your mutation-placement rule) and scored against your goals (length, Tm, GC %, G/C cap — all editable). The mutation can sit inside the overlap (both primers carry it, QuikChange-like) or in the 3′ extension of one primer (the overlap stays wild-type, with ≥ "Min matched flank" matched bases between the overlap and the mutation). The 3′ extension is length-tuned to the target annealing Tm. A G/C cap means the overlap starts and ends with G/C, so the 3′ ends that anneal after exonuclease chew-back are GC-clamped.

Simple inverse PCR. The two primers are back-to-back (divergent) at a junction in the template and carry no additional DNA — each is purely annealing sequence. The mutation sits inside one primer with ≥ "Min matched flank" matched bases on both sides. Arms are length-tuned to the target Tm, and junctions are ranked by Tm match between the two primers and a 3′ G/C clamp. The product is a blunt linear amplicon: order the primers 5′-phosphorylated (or phosphorylate with PNK) and re-circularize with blunt ligation / KLD after DpnI digestion.

Tm uses SantaLucia 1998 nearest-neighbor ΔH/ΔS with Owczarzy 2008 monovalent+divalent salt correction; free Mg²⁺ = [Mg²⁺] − [dNTP]. Annealing Tm is evaluated on the primer's own bases (the perfect-match Tm against the product made in cycle 2+); the reported 5′/3′ matched flanks are the runs of perfectly-matched bases on each side of the mutation that pair with the original template in cycle 1.
